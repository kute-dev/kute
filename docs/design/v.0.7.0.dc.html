cert-manager — the issuance chain

Issuers, ClusterIssuers, CertificateRequests: generic registry kinds (spec 14a), no new screens. What earns chrome: the Certificate ↵ resolves the request → order → challenge chain and promotes the deepest failure — plus an expiry column and one renew verb, all registry data.

  • A stuck cert is the canonical scattered-objects problem: the answer lives three hops away, Certificate → CertificateRequest → Order → Challenge. Kute walks ownerRefs from the watch and promotes the deepest failure message, verbatim — same move as pod detail putting last-termination first (spec 5a).
  • The chain rows are selectable: `↵` lands on the object's generic CRD detail / YAML (spec 14d) — so Orders, Challenges, and CertificateRequests never need destination screens of their own. They stay generic registry kinds, reached through the chain.
  • For non-ACME issuers the chain is just two rows (cert → request) — the recipe degrades, no special casing. A healthy cert shows a green strip and no failure card: zero chrome until earned.
  • The refs strip resolves the target Secret (secret semantics from spec 21a) and the Issuer/ClusterIssuer, with live existence + Ready state — `↵` jumps to either.
  • Same skeleton as every detail view: header bar · failure card · rows · strip · keybar. Nothing here is a describe page.

CERTS ✓ renewal requested · admin-tls ↵ watch the chain
  • Renew is reversible tier — cert-manager reissues into a new secret and keeps serving the old one until it's ready — so it gets the inline will-run line, not a modal. Friction scales with risk.
  • The will-run line shows the patch Kute actually applies — an Issuing condition on the Certificate status subresource. Not `cmctl renew`: cmctl is what a human would type, but Kute doesn't shell out to it, and printing a command the user may not have installed breaks the will-run contract. What's shown is what runs, and `y` copies exactly that.
  • After `↵` the row flips to issuing in place and the keybar confirms — no toast, no navigation. `↵` again opens the chain (35a) to watch it land.
  • Works identically from the list (35b) and the chain view. On a PROD context, renew stays reversible tier — it's delete that escalates, not reissue.
